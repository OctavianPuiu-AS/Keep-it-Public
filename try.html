    Things to try

    1. Capture a setup

      Use the controls on the picture: hide the chairs, tap a colour dot, swing the sun. Then press the big Keep it button. The whole look is saved as one capture.

    2. Restore it

      Change everything again, then use the arrows under the picture, or tap the picture to see every capture. The scene goes straight back.

    3. Go back with Latest

      Restored the wrong one? The clock at the top right is Latest: the scene goes back to how it was just before that click. Press it again to return to the capture.

    4. Capture only part of it

      The icons on the left choose what Keep it saves and restores. Switch the Lights icon off, then restore: your lamps stay as they are. “Just the tables” only holds visibility and materials, so it leaves your lights and sky alone.

    5. Save a view

      Drag the picture to orbit, then press Save view. Views are camera angles. With the Camera icon on, a capture also keeps the view it was kept from, and restoring it moves there.

    6. Render

      On the right, pick the captures and the views you want, then press Render. Each capture is drawn from each view. In Blender this is a render job: real renders, saved to disk, while you get on with something else.

    7. Change one picture

      Click any picture in the render. The scene jumps to it: change a colour, the sun or the angle, then Save to picture. Only that picture changes; the capture stays as it was.

    In Blender

    The playground keeps four things about a handful of objects. The add-on works on your whole scene:

    • A capture holds much more than colours and a light.
    • Views are your real cameras, and can be animated.
    • Render jobs write real images, every capture from every view.
