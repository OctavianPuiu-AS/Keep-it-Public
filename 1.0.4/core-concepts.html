The vocabulary

Keep it has a small vocabulary. Once these seven ideas click, the whole add-on makes sense.

Capture
A saved state of your scene — its lights, materials, visibility, world, render settings, and more. You choose which of these categories each capture includes. Restoring a capture brings that state back in one click, without touching anything you didn’t save. Think of captures as the different looks or versions of your scene.
View
A saved camera angle and framing — a still shot, or an animated sequence. Views are completely independent from captures, so any view can be combined with any capture. Think of views as the shots you want to see your scene from.
Render Job
A pairing of captures with views. Select two captures and two views, and the job becomes four shots (every capture seen through every view). This is where captures and views come together into actual output.
Variant
An option that multiplies a job’s output — resolution, file format, frame range, frame rate, colour management, compositor, or material colorway. Add two resolutions to a four-shot job and you get eight renders. Variants let one job cover every technical version you need without building separate jobs.
Colorway
A named set of material swaps: on this object, in this slot, use this material instead. Applied before a shot renders and reversed after, so the same capture can render in three fabrics without duplicating anything.
Render Profile
A reusable bundle of render settings (engine, sampling, colour management, output format, passes) that you author in Blender’s own Render Properties and assign to a job. Set your quality once, reuse it across every job.
Cell
One rendered image: a single capture, seen through a single view, with one combination of variants. A job is a list of shots. The contact sheet shows each one as a cell you can preview.

Two things worth knowing beyond the core seven

  • Scope — a capture doesn’t have to cover the whole scene. You can scope it to the selected objects, a chosen list of objects, or specific collections — or invert that with Exclude, capturing everything else instead. It only saves and restores that part.
  • Latest — before you browse away from unsaved work, Keep it automatically tucks the live scene into a “Latest” slot, so you can always click back to exactly where you were.
